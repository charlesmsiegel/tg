Judge 1

Theory: This package models a player's identity and standing in a tabletop-RPG social structure: who they are (a User plus preferences like theme and content safety tools 'lines'/'veils'), and what authority they hold over a given chronicle (player who owns characters/items/locations, or storyteller who approves and adjudicates them). Profile is the single hub a person logs into to see 'what's mine' and 'what needs my attention'.

Instead of: The other coherent reading is a thin 'user identity + preferences' app, with storyteller relationships living entirely inside `game` (where the STRelationship model actually resides) and each domain app (characters/items/locations/game) owning its own approval and XP-award views. Instead the code fused everything into Profile/ProfileView as one dashboard: Profile methods reach into characters, items, locations, mage.Rote and game to build 'to-approve' and 'my-stuff' queries, and accounts/views.py owns approval, freebie, and XP-award endpoints for objects it doesn't otherwise touch. That's a defensible UX choice (one profile page as the action hub) but it was never stated as the app's actual scope anywhere, and the checked-in README still describes the untaken, separated-dashboard path (approval_queue.html, dashboard.html) instead of the fused one that was actually built.

Judge 2

Theory: This package models a player's personal control panel for a play-by-post tabletop chronicle: their account preferences and safety-tool settings (theme, Discord contact, 'lines and veils' content boundaries), and their standing in the storyteller hierarchy (head ST, relationship ST, or plain player) that determines what they can review and approve. It doubles as the place a person looks to see everything currently waiting on them across the whole game - characters, items, locations, images, rotes, and XP - whether they are the submitter or the approver.

Instead of: A thinner reading would keep Profile to identity/preferences plus a narrow authorization primitive (is_st/is_st_for), and put cross-app 'what's pending for me' aggregation in a separate dashboard or notification service that each app feeds via its own queryset methods. The code didn't take that path: Profile itself became the aggregation root, importing directly from characters, items, locations and game (accounts/models.py:6-13) and growing ~15 dashboard-query methods (my_characters, *_to_approve, *_images_to_approve, xp_* ) alongside its actual preference fields.

Judge 3

Theory: A Profile is one person's control panel for the game: their cosmetic/communication preferences (theme, heading font, Discord visibility, lines/veils), plus a live, chronicle-scoped account of what that person is owed or owes as a storyteller or player — who they run games for (via STRelationship), and which characters/items/locations/rotes/images/XP requests are waiting on their approval or submission right now. It answers two real-world questions at once: 'how do I want this app to look and behave for me' and 'what's in my inbox today, and by what authority.'

Instead of: The other coherent reading would keep Profile a thin preferences record (theme/heading/discord/lines/veils — nothing else) and push every 'what's pending for this user' query into the owning app (game, characters, items, locations) as manager methods or a dedicated Dashboard/Inbox service, composed only at the view/context-processor layer. The code didn't take that path: instead Profile itself grew ~20 query methods (my_characters, *_to_approve, xp_*, unread_scenes, get_unfulfilled_weekly_xp_requests...) importing from characters, items, locations, and game directly (accounts/models.py:6-13), so a settings model became the de facto cross-app aggregation root. That choice buys a single ergonomic entry point (`request.user.profile.foo()`) for templates and the context processor, at the cost of Profile now depending on nearly every other app's status vocabulary (Un/Sub/App/Ret/Dec) and being the place three unrelated concerns (UI prefs, ST authority, approval queues) collide.
