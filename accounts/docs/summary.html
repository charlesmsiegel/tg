accounts is small by line count (~3,500 lines of Python plus templates) but sits at the center of the app: its Profile model is where every other app's "what needs my attention" data — pending characters, items, locations, rotes, images, freebies, and weekly/scene XP — gets aggregated into a storyteller's dashboard and notification badge. The package itself owns almost no domain logic; it authorizes (is this user an ST for this chronicle?) and lists (what's waiting?), while the actual approval writes live in core/services/approval.py and the actual "am I an ST" data lives in game's STRelationship/Chronicle models. That thinness is exactly why it reads cleanly at the architecture level and yet still has a real, verifiable authorization/visibility gap and a stale-choice-list bug underneath — both are quick to spot once you know the two or three query methods that fan out across the rest of the codebase.
